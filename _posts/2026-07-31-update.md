### Cool Science
There's another eclipse on August 12, which will include a short period (~2 min) of totality. It's interesting <a href="https://www.universetoday.com/articles/into-totality-our-guide-to-the-august-12th-total-solar-eclipse" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">seeing the travel of the eclipse across Earth</a>, as it's such a precise shadow. It always appears to be such a colossal scale, but in the grand scheme of things it's just a little dot travelling across a curved surface. I have an <a href="https://luciferread.com/eclipse-tracker/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">eclipse tracker</a> on my website that updates when Earth will have its next total eclipse based on NASA's calculations. The tool also tracks Mars and Jupiter, which are predicted based on mathematical calculations. There’s also no easy way to prove me wrong so you’ll just have to take my word for it. It is interesting how much more frequent eclipses are elsewhere, but that's a story for another day...
